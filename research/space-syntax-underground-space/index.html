Rethinking Underground Space Utilization in Metro Stations: A Case Study of Shanghai's Jing'an Temple

Graduate Course Project · Tongji University · 2024–2025 · Team Lead

Highlights

  1. Integrated Space Syntax with field surveys and agent-based simulation to evaluate underground-space performance.
  2. Linked spatial configuration and visibility with pedestrian flow, spatial efficiency, and user experience.
  3. Translated analytical findings into planning and commercial-space strategies for circulation, wayfinding, and high-visibility facility placement.

Overview

This project investigates how the underground public and commercial spaces around Shanghai's Jing'an Temple Metro Station can be evaluated and optimized using Space Syntax and data-driven analysis. Integration, Choice, and Visibility Graph Analysis were used to assess accessibility, spatial centrality, and visual accessibility, while pedestrian-flow surveys and agent-based simulations were used to examine actual and simulated movement patterns. The results were translated into spatial-design strategies aimed at improving pedestrian circulation, spatial legibility, user experience, and the placement of commercial functions.

My Contribution

  • Led the team and coordinated the overall analytical workflow and final presentation.
  • Conducted and interpreted Space Syntax analyses, including Integration, Choice, and visibility-based measures.
  • Translated findings into planning and design recommendations for underground public and commercial spaces.
