Quantifying Heat-Adjusted Health Benefits of Cycling in Cities

National Key R&D Program of China · Tongji University · 2025–2026 · Advisors: Prof. Lan Wang; Asst. Prof. Wenyao Sun

Highlights

  1. Developed a Heat-Exposure Adjusted Cycling Health Benefit Index (HCBI) that integrates physical-activity benefits and heat-exposure risks in a unified framework.
  2. Quantified HCBI at a high spatial resolution (100 m) across central Shanghai, and identified spatially vulnerable areas where cycling benefits are disproportionately offset by heat risks.

Overview

This study examines how much of cycling’s health benefit remains after accounting for heat exposure. We developed the Heat-Exposure Adjusted Cycling Health Benefit Index (HCBI), which places cycling-related physical-activity benefits and heat-related health risks within a unified comparable framework. Using cycling trajectories, land-surface temperature, and built-environment data, HCBI was estimated at 100 m resolution across Shanghai. Machine-learning analyses were then used to examine how urban form shapes net health outcomes, revealing both trade-offs and nonlinear effects relevant to climate-resilient cycling and urban design.

My Contribution

  • Co-developed the HCBI framework and formulation.
  • Processed and integrated multi-source data at 100 m resolution for estimating HCBI across Shanghai.
  • Contributed to drafting the equal-contribution manuscript and the related patent application.
